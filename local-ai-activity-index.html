    Methodology · Version 1.0 beta

    A useful signal, with a visible boundary.

    LocalClaw Atlas is being built in public. The beta starts with a transparent measure we actually have: anonymous, aggregated interest in LocalClaw by country and a privacy-thresholded U.S. state drill-down. Verified installations and active local model runs will only appear after a separate, opt-in telemetry system exists.

    What counts today?

    A country-level unique website visitor reported by DataFast during the 30-day window. For the United States, the same source also supplies an approximate region or state. Both are interest signals.

    What does not count?

    No installation, model launch, prompt, inference, machine identity, or claim about the total number of local AI users is included in this beta.

    How are locations ranked?

    Countries and published U.S. states are ordered by raw observed signals. Dots are deterministic visual samples constrained to the correct country or state polygon; they are not exact visitor coordinates. The ranking is not normalized for population, internet access, or LocalClaw market reach.

    How is privacy handled?

    The public artifact contains aggregated totals only. Countries and U.S. states below five signals are withheld from the globe, and no person-level data is published.

    Source and coverage: aggregated anonymous LocalClaw website traffic measured by DataFast, 31 July–29 August 2026, Europe/Zurich. U.S. boundaries come from the U.S. Census Bureau 2024 Cartographic Boundary Files. Source coverage is biased toward people and network routes that reach LocalClaw.

    Questions, answered plainly

    What the index can—and cannot—tell you.

    Which country leads the current snapshot?

    The United States leads this 30-day LocalClaw interest snapshot with 1,309 signals. That result reflects this dataset, not all local AI activity worldwide.

    What does “local AI” mean here?

    AI models designed to run on personal devices or privately controlled hardware instead of relying entirely on a third-party inference API.

    Is this a local AI adoption ranking?

    Not yet. Adoption requires trustworthy installation or active-use signals and a defensible population denominator. The beta deliberately reports interest instead.

    What comes next?

    With explicit opt-in telemetry, Atlas can add verified installed and active views, then compare raw activity with a separate per-capita measure.

    Can I explore individual states?

    Yes. Select the United States, then choose any published state. State boundaries are official, while signal locations remain approximate network geolocation rather than verified residence.
